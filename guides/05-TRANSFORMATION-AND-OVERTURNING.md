# Transformation and Overturning

*Circulation moves water through space. Transformation moves water through
temperature, salinity, and density classes.*

## Two coordinate systems

```text
GEOGRAPHIC VIEW                       PROPERTY-SPACE VIEW

Atlantic ─► Nordic ─► Arctic         warm/salty class
              │                            │ cooling + freshening
              ▼                            ▼
         dense outflow                cold/dense class
              │                            │ formation/export
              ▼                            ▼
        North Atlantic deep           another class volume
```

Both describe the same ocean, but they preserve different relationships.

## Object cards

| Object/process | Defined by |
|---|---|
| Water-mass transformation | volume flux across a temperature, salinity, or density coordinate |
| Formation | convergence of transformation into a class, increasing its volume |
| Subduction | transfer from the surface mixed layer into the ocean interior |
| Overturning | circulation viewed as exchange between upper/lower or light/dense branches |
| Mixing | irreversible blending that changes property distributions and gradients |
| Heave | displacement of property surfaces without necessarily changing water properties |

## The Walin idea

Surface heat and freshwater fluxes change buoyancy where density classes meet
the atmosphere. Interior mixing also transfers volume between classes. The
Walin framework balances those transformations against transport and changing
class volume.

[Chen et al. 2025](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2025JC022445)
defines transformation as volume flux from one specified state to another.
[Desbruyères et al. 2025](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2025GL117970)
uses the balance among surface forcing, mixing, export, and local volume change.

## Why the conveyor belt is insufficient

```text
OLD PICTURE                           MODERN PICTURE

one red ribbon ─────────►             branches ─┬─► recirculation
                    │                           ├─► eddies/interior paths
one blue ribbon ◄───┘                           ├─► transformation
                                                └─► variable return routes
```

The conveyor remains a useful first sketch, but modern reviews replace its
laminar path with intricate, eddy-rich, time-varying systems
([Tailleux 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41612-025-01185-8)).

## Nordic and Arctic example

Warm, salty Atlantic Water crosses the Greenland–Scotland Ridge, cools as it
moves poleward, and divides between Fram Strait, the Barents Sea, and
recirculations. Current 2026 research finds different heat retention and
transformation in those branches
([Koenigk et al. 2026](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/22/2375/2026/))
and emphasizes Arctic pathways, surface forcing, and timescales
([Arctic overturning circulation 2026](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/22/565/2026/)).

## The decisive tests

1. Choose the coordinate: temperature, salinity, neutral density, or another tracer.
2. Separate reversible heave from irreversible property change.
3. Include surface forcing, interior mixing, transport, and storage consistently.
4. Track where classes outcrop and where observations actually sample them.
5. Test sensitivity to bins and equation-of-state choices.

## Common mistakes

- Calling geographic cooling alone “water-mass formation.”
- Assuming sinking at one point supplies a continuous deep conveyor path.
- Equating convection with net mass transport.
- Forgetting that heat and freshwater can push density in different directions.
- Treating density classes as material labels that survive mixing forever.

## OSW next measurement
