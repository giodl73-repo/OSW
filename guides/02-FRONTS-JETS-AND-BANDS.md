# Fronts, Jets, and Bands

*A front is a boundary. A jet is a fast current. They often travel together,
but they are not the same object.*

## Object cards

| Object | Defined by | Dimension | Carries transport? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front | sharp horizontal property gradient | line at the surface; surface in 3-D | not by definition |
| Jet | narrow local maximum of directed velocity | elongated ribbon or tube | usually, but quantity must be measured |
| Band | visually or statistically persistent stripe | region | not necessarily |
| Current | organized mean flow with recognized pathway | pathway/volume | yes, though heat depends on properties |

## Why fronts and jets coincide

```text
                 sea surface
 cold, dense water       │       warm, light water
 ███████████████████████ │ ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░
          pressure slope │
                        FRONT
                          ║
                          ║  JET flows along the front →→→
                          ╚═══════════════════════════════
```

A lateral density gradient creates a pressure gradient. Under rotation,
geostrophic balance turns much of the flow along rather than directly across
the front. Fronts can therefore sharpen jets, while eddies generated by unstable
jets can reshape the fronts.

## A hierarchy, not a fixed count

```text
broad current system
└── frontal current band
    ├── persistent jet
    │   └── energetic core
    └── recirculating branch
```

The count depends on resolution, averaging interval, depth, and detection rule.
A recent Southern Ocean review notes that there is no uniquely accepted
definition of eddies, jets, or fronts and often treats them together as
mesoscale geostrophic turbulence
([Bennetts et al. 2024](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2022RG000781)).

## Examples

- **Gulf Stream:** western boundary current with strong fronts, a swift core,
  meanders, rings, and recirculations.
- **Antarctic Circumpolar Current:** circumpolar current system containing
  multiple interacting fronts and jets rather than one uniform river.
- **Norwegian Atlantic Current system:** slope and frontal branches carrying
  Atlantic Water poleward through the Nordic Seas.
- **West Spitsbergen Current:** an Arctic gateway current with a core, offshore
  branch, and recirculations seen in modern transects
  ([von Appen et al. 2026](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/22/1003/2026/)).

## How to detect them

### Front tests

- temperature, salinity, density, sea-surface-height, or ocean-colour gradient;
- persistence and position uncertainty;
- vertical continuation below the surface;
- sensitivity to threshold and smoothing scale.

### Jet tests

- local maximum of along-path velocity;
- coherent direction over a declared width and depth;
- persistence across time;
- transport integrated across a perpendicular section.

The first global daily front dataset spanning 1982–2023 demonstrates how mature
front mapping has become, but it remains an atlas of one object family
([Gao et al. 2025](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/2831/2025/index.html)).

## OSW example

At the Iceland–Scotland boundary, one face remains the strongest absolute heat
transporter in every month of 2018. Four adjacent faces form a compact +106.5 TW
core. Three-face or approximately 20 km smoothing preserves that core; broader

## Common mistakes

- Drawing one arrow and calling it the current.
- Treating temperature gradient as velocity.
- Treating velocity magnitude as heat transport.
- Naming every local maximum as an independent jet.
- Hiding the spatial and temporal scale at which a jet was defined.
