# Literature review: ocean-heat geography citation spine

## Phase 1 — Claims requiring support

1. **Storage, transport, and anomaly must remain separate.** Confirmation requires observing-system and heat-content methodology; a skeptic would cite products that use temperature proxies successfully and ask whether the distinction is operationally necessary.
2. **Southern Ocean fronts and slope systems regulate but do not stop poleward exchange.** Confirmation requires hydrography, altimetry, direct eddy-flux observations, and modern synthesis; a skeptic would cite persistent mean fronts as evidence for a stronger barrier metaphor.
3. **Drake Passage geometry affects climate, but gateway state alone does not uniquely determine Antarctic climate or ice response.** Confirmation requires controlled counterfactual models, paleoclimate attribution, and chronology; a skeptic would cite classic thermal-isolation narratives or a single closure experiment as deterministic.
4. **Surface SST, anomaly, and estimated error cannot diagnose subsurface heat storage or delivery.** Confirmation requires ocean-observing-system design, full-depth heat-content methods, and documented cases where surface and subsurface evolution differ; a skeptic would cite the spatial completeness and practical utility of satellite-derived surface fields.

## Phase 2 — What the literature says

### Per-source interrogation

1. **Orsi, Whitworth & Nowlin (1995):** foundational hydrographic definition of ACC fronts; supports Claim 2, while its climatological paths cannot make fronts fixed material contours.
2. **Sokolov & Rintoul (2009):** altimetry, hydrography, and Argo show multiple circumpolar jets that merge, diverge, and respond to bathymetry; supports Claim 2 and weakens a single-wall reading.
3. **Marshall & Speer (2012):** theoretical synthesis of Southern Ocean upwelling and overturning closure; supports the mechanism-level framing in Claims 1 and 2.
4. **England et al. (2017):** coupled Drake-closure experiment emphasizes ice–atmosphere feedbacks; supports Claim 3 as a counterfactual, not a feasible intervention.
5. **Goldner, Herold & Huber (2014):** finds Antarctic glaciation/CO₂ forcing can drive circulation change at the Eocene–Oligocene transition; directly challenges gateway-only causation in Claim 3.
6. **Thompson et al. (2018):** review of Antarctic Slope Current and cross-slope exchange; supports the regional, process-dependent barrier hierarchy in Claim 2.
7. **Spence et al. (2014):** poleward-wind-shift experiment produces rapid, geographically structured subsurface coastal warming; supports Claims 2 and 4 while remaining model-dependent.
8. **Gutierrez-Villanueva, Chereskin & Sprintall (2020):** observational upper-ocean eddy heat flux across the ACC in Drake Passage; directly supports the “leaky front” qualification in Claim 2.
9. **Bennetts et al. (2024):** holistic Southern Ocean review connects circulation, cryosphere, turbulence, and waves and emphasizes unresolved linkages; supports Claims 1, 2, and 4.
10. **Rosevear et al. (2025):** review of the successive barriers and boundary processes between ocean heat and ice-shelf melting; supports the non-deterministic boundary in Claims 2 and 3.
11. **Palmer et al. (2019):** observing-system adequacy review shows regional heat storage requires subsurface coverage and uncertainty-aware sampling; supports Claims 1 and 4.
12. **Meyssignac et al. (2019):** global ocean-heat-content methodology documents the central role and limits of in-situ profile observations and reanalyses; supports Claims 1 and 4.
13. **Gentemann, Fewings & García-Reyes (2017):** satellite characterization of the 2014–2016 Northeast Pacific marine heatwave; supports anomaly-as-event language but remains surface-specific under Claim 4.
14. **Jackson et al. (2018):** observes marine-heatwave-related warming lingering at depth in a British Columbia fjord; shows why surface evolution cannot stand in for subsurface persistence under Claim 4.
15. **Hodel et al. (2021):** reconstructs Drake gateway/ACC chronology and reconsiders neodymium evidence; supports chronology relevance but also the uncertainty and multi-cause boundary in Claim 3.
