# Web grounding — biological and chemical ocean objects

## Claims to ground

| # | Claim | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Concentration, vertically integrated inventory/pool, and flux are different claims. | Their dimensions and map meanings cannot be exchanged. |
| 2 | A persistent oxygen-minimum zone differs from a thresholded hypoxic or anoxic event. | Natural structure and event classification need different identity tests. |
| 3 | Chlorophyll, phytoplankton biomass, primary production, bloom, and harmful bloom are non-equivalent. | Ocean colour alone cannot identify productivity, species, toxin, or harm. |
| 4 | DIC, alkalinity, pH, pCO2, carbonate saturation, and air–sea CO2 flux are distinct carbon-system variables. | A single “carbon blob” would be chemically and dimensionally false. |
| 5 | Biological and chemical objects are carried and transformed by physical circulation without becoming currents or provinces. | Guide 12 must connect to—not replace—the physical grammar. |

## Evidence by claim

### Claim 1 — concentration, pool, and flux differ

- Query: `ocean carbon concentration inventory air sea flux distinction`
  - Source: <https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/60997/noaa_60997_DS1.pdf>
  - Direct quote: “concentration changes were vertically integrated”
  - Relevance: inventory requires integration over a declared water volume.
- Query: `GOOS biogeochemistry essential ocean variables oxygen nutrients inorganic carbon`
  - Source: <https://goosocean.org/what-we-do/framework/essential-ocean-variables/>
  - Direct quote: “minimum set of ocean variables”
  - Relevance: GOOS treats oxygen, nutrients, carbon, and biology as specified variables, not one object class.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

### Claim 2 — OMZ, hypoxia, and anoxia differ

- Query: `site:noaa.gov oxygen minimum zone persistent layer definition`
  - Source: <https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/ocean-fact/omz/>
  - Direct quote: “persistent layers in the water column”
  - Relevance: persistence and vertical structure define the OMZ concept.
- Query: `site:noaa.gov hypoxia dissolved oxygen threshold ocean`
  - Source: <https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/archive/archive-management-system/OAS/bin/prd/jquery/datatype/details/743>
  - Direct quote: “below 2 mg/L”
  - Relevance: NOAA's coastal hypoxia term is thresholded, not synonymous with every oxygen minimum.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

### Claim 3 — chlorophyll, production, bloom, and harm differ

- Query: `site:noaa.gov chlorophyll proxy phytoplankton biomass primary production`
  - Source: <https://www.integratedecosystemassessment.noaa.gov/regions/northeast/northeast-phytoplankton>
  - Direct quote: “used as a proxy for phytoplankton biomass”
  - Relevance: chlorophyll is not a direct census and production is separately modeled.
- Query: `site:noaa.gov not all algal blooms harmful`
  - Source: <https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/habharm.html>
  - Direct quote: “not all algal blooms are harmful”
  - Relevance: harmful status requires species, toxin, or impact evidence.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

### Claim 4 — carbon variables and flux differ

- Query: `site:noaa.gov pH pCO2 DIC total alkalinity four parameters`
  - Source: <https://www.pmel.noaa.gov/co2/story/Laboratory%2BAnalysis>
  - Direct quote: “four measurable parameters”
  - Relevance: pH, DIC, TA, and pCO2 constrain one coupled system but remain different variables.
- Query: `site:ioccp.org DIC alkalinity pH pCO2 carbonate system`
  - Source: <https://www.ioccp.org/images/03TimeSeries/BatesN14.pdf>
  - Direct quote: “DIC is the sum”
  - Relevance: DIC is a concentration sum of carbonate species, not air–sea exchange.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

### Claim 5 — biology and chemistry overlap physical structure

- Query: `NOAA oxygen minimum zone physical biological chemical processes circulation`
  - Source: <https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/ocean-fact/omz/>
  - Direct quote: “biological, chemical, and physical processes”
  - Relevance: OMZ identity emerges from interacting mechanisms rather than one current.
- Query: `NOAA phytoplankton primary production currents temperature nutrients bloom`
  - Source: <https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/education/tutorial-coastal/harmful-algal-blooms/habs01.html>
  - Direct quote: “tides and currents”
  - Relevance: circulation affects bloom scope but does not define harmfulness.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

## Ungrounded claims

No ungrounded claims. Exact global detection thresholds and external vocabulary
identifiers remain implementation work.

## Classification amendments

1. Add persistent oxygen-minimum structure separately from hypoxic/anoxic events.
2. Add deep chlorophyll maximum separately from surface chlorophyll proxy,
   phytoplankton community, production, and bloom.
3. Represent nutrient and DIC pools as analyst-integrated budget constructs;
   represent air–sea CO2 exchange as a surface flux.
4. Add harmful algal bloom with an impact classifier rather than assuming high
   chlorophyll or visible colour establishes harm.
5. Add primary production and biological carbon pump as processes.

## Search improvements

1. A map contract should select exact GOOS/CF variables and units before data acquisition.
2. Regional oxygen and bloom thresholds must cite the biological or management context.
3. Carbon implementations must record scale, temperature, salinity, pressure,
   calculation constants, gas-transfer formulation, and uncertainty.
