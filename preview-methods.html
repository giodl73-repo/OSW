Keep the source and scope with the number.

OSW brings named ocean features, approximate map routes, and source-scoped measurement references into one atlas. It is an independent project, with evolving coverage.

What is in this preview?

The edition retains 111 current identities and 40 named-eddy source records. It joins 941 scoped depth references: 937 in metres and four in pressure units, dbar. A separate eddy-evidence collection retains 331 references, including 203 size references. These are reference counts, not counts of distinct physical eddies or complete current profiles.

Some identities are families, systems, or recurrent regions. Their members do not automatically inherit each other’s measurements. Ten current family/system entries remain unassessed for depth in this snapshot; there are no whole-route depth profiles or physical current-volume estimates.

What does a map mark mean?

A locator gives a starting place for reading. An editorial route is an approximate path drawn with stated conventions. The map card names each available role; an unlocated record remains accessible by name. OSW’s colored ocean states are editorial regions, not political territories or fixed current boundaries.

The world map uses an equirectangular longitude–latitude grid. Its apparent widths and areas distort toward the poles. Use the evidence records for dimensions. A region association can describe a point or an undated reference-curve crossing; it does not automatically establish passage of a physical current or containment of an eddy.

How should I compare measurements?

Start with the metric, unit, place, date, and method. A reported reach, an approximate route distance, a velocity-threshold width, a water-mass thickness, and an instrument depth answer different questions. Metres and dbar remain separate; pressure has not been converted to depth.

The explorer orders only a selected, stated scalar within one exact metric and unit. It does not invent a midpoint for a range. Source disagreements, spatial ranges, standard deviations, uncertainty intervals, and censored limits retain their own definitions. A missing value means unknown or unassessed, not zero.

There is no general multiplication of length, width, and depth into volume: those dimensions must describe compatible places, times, and boundaries first. Seasonal or dated references do not establish a complete annual cycle.

Why separate eddy references from eddy identities?

A paper can supply a useful size estimate without establishing that its event is the same event as an entry in a name registry. The separate source-reference collection keeps those facts available while leaving the identity join unverified. A figure reading, model diagnostic, contour proxy, radius, and diameter remain different kinds of evidence.

Original association and eligibility qualifications are preserved in exact records. Some describe the earlier named-registry admission scope; they are not silently promoted by this preview’s separate factual-use decisions. Unresolved registry identity records, structured registry labels and dates, and held provider material are withheld.

Follow the evidence

Cards and explorer rows link to retained primary paper or data attribution and exact reference records. An OSW admission-method citation explains how a reference entered the inventory; it is not an additional independent observation. Source data can have its own access and reuse conditions.

NASA views and map credits

The 70 NASA navigation views remain hosted by NASA and show modeled ocean motion. Regional matches use display overlap with OSW’s 56 editorial region names. A crop does not establish that NASA identified a particular named current or eddy. OSW is not endorsed by NASA.
