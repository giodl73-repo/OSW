# Gulf Stream published width definitions

2026-10-14. Two independent regional definitions attach to `gulf-stream`, not
the larger `gulf-stream-system`, Florida Current or North Atlantic Current.
They supplement the existing inverse-model cruise section; they do not define
a comparable whole-current width, geographic footprint or annual extrema.

1. Retain the institutional originals of Richardson and Knauss (1971),
   doi:10.1016/0011-7471(71)90095-7, and Lozier, Owens and Curry (1995),
   doi:10.1016/0079-6611(95)00013-5, locally with exact acquisition checksums.
   Do not redistribute their PDFs, figures or digitized curves. Attribute factual
   numerical extractions and draw independent graphics.
2. Richardson/Knauss printed1094–1095 (PDF6–7) explicitly reports widths145,
   140 and120 km for the three complete transport-float crossings; printed1107
   (PDF19) reports their mean135 km. Figure2 (PDF5) and Table2 (PDF17) identify
   the complete crossings as1,3,4, not1,2,3. Crossing2 is partial and gets no width.
3. Preserve the complete-crossing periods as date ranges: 1967-06-30 to07-05,
   07-11 to07-15 and07-16 to07-18. The source clock timezone is unspecified;
   do not turn figure clock labels into UTC timestamps or single-day snapshots.
   The full campaign26June–18July is separate from the three width occupations.
4. Width is measured along the fixed135-degree-true baseline, between the
   author-smoothed downstream transport curve's zero crossings. Velocity is
   the component normal to the baseline, integrated surface to local bottom.
   This is not a surface half-speed or exactly flow-normal width. Baseline
   bearing135 degrees and reported mean135 km are different quantities.
5. Retain the source extrapolation: no observed negative inshore transport;
   authors extend to zero near the shallow shelf. Two complete crossings have
   offshore negative values; the other is also extended to zero. These are
   published source-specific assumptions, not permission for OSW's automatic
   component operator to close missing edges. Exact geographic edges, width
   error and fixed depth-layer bounds remain null. A3000 m narrowing is context.
6. The finite120–145 km occupation span and mean135 km form one source record
   with three subordinate occupations. Do not count the mean as a fourth
   independent occupation, infer seasonal/annual limits or set uncertainty from
   the occupation spread, transport error, Loran precision or instrument error.
7. Lozier printed17/PDF17 reports about300 km near70 W, estimated from the
   separation of100 and400 dbar pressure contours on the sigma-theta26.50
   surface (section5.1/Figure7). These are pressure contours on an isopycnal,
   not fixed100–400 m depth limits or velocity thresholds. Exact geographic
   boundary coordinates and direction of measurement are not extracted.
8. Keep its historical climatological support distinct from the1967 occupations.
   The archive was available through April1990; that cutoff is not a precise
   local averaging period. Section2 gives the basin-wide database, not the
   dates contributing to this local contour span. Observed period stays null.
9. The source attributes broadening to meandering and discusses a500 km
   meander envelope and100 km smoothing scale. Neither is a second width or
   uncertainty interval. Do not infer a150 km instantaneous record from the
   approximate factor-of-two comparison or form a135–300 km annual range.
10. Preserve both definitions, their source assumptions and the existing256 km
    inverse-model section separately. Allow source-year/occupation inspection
    and independent graphics; do not assign geographic width bands, state-area
    intersections, monthly playback, whole-system dimensions or scientific rank.
