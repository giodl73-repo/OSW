# Current-length source triage

Status: research worklist, updated 2026-10-02. The ranked almanac contains eleven
source-reported approximate estimates. The remaining current records should
not be assigned a number solely because a source reports a nearby coastline,
ecosystem, survey box, cross-stream width, or partial study reach.

| Current or family | Tempting number in a source | Why it is not a whole-current length | Next admissible evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| North Atlantic Current | The [AMS Glossary](https://glossary.ametsoc.org/wiki/north-atlantic-current/) says it initially follows the subtropical gyre for less than 500 km before turning northeast. | The number describes an initial branch before the longer flow toward the northeast Atlantic. | A source-defined start, downstream branch, and whole-branch path estimate; keep Norwegian and Irminger continuations distinct. |
| Brazil Current | [Tomczak and Godfrey, Chapter 14](https://incois.gov.in/Tutor/regoc/pdffiles/colour/single/14P-Atlantic.pdf) describe the first 1,500 km of strengthening from near 10°S. A [UN/GEF regional assessment](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/570896/files/giwaregional39.pdf) gives 4,150 km for a coastal management region called “Brazil Current.” | The first is a partial reach; the second is a managed coastline, not a current path. The current separates or meets the Malvinas Current at a variable latitude. | A physical-current endpoint convention and a sourced path estimate for that convention. |
| Benguela Current | A [marine planning paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7487156/) gives about 6,800 km for the Benguela Current Large Marine Ecosystem coastline. | An ecosystem coast can include adjacent seas and is not the current axis. A [2023 circulation analysis](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/19/1009/2023/) reports section variability and no clear continuity north of 19°S in its analyzed flow. | A current-specific alongflow reach or two source-defined gates with continuity support; label any resulting geometry separately from the ecosystem. |
| Kuroshio Extension | [KESS field surveys](https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/gsofacpubs/1222/) cover an approximately 600 × 600 km study region around the first meander trough. | Study-area dimensions and cross-jet sections do not determine the extension's downstream path or endpoint. | A dated or climatological path with a declared downstream stopping rule. |
| Labrador Current | The [AMS Glossary](https://glossary.ametsoc.org/wiki/labrador-current/) describes flow toward the Grand Banks and a variable polar front but supplies no along-current length. | The name covers shelf, slope, and recirculating branches; an iceberg's southward reach is not a current length. | Separate the coastal and slope branches, then seek source-defined endpoints or a published branch length. |

For each candidate, record the exact source passage, object identity, upstream
and downstream gates, time/depth convention, and whether the number is a
published estimate, a bounded sampled reach, or an OSW geodesic floor. A
source-reported number enters the ranked inventory only after its scope can be
stated beside it. This worklist does not assert that no suitable source exists;
it records why the specific tempting numbers above were excluded.

## Admitted: Antarctic Slope Current

The inventory now includes Antarctic Slope Current as its 100th record. The existing
Antarctic Coastal Current and Antarctic Circumpolar Current do not absorb
this name: Thompson et al. (2018) distinguish them in section 1.1 of
[The Antarctic Slope Current in a Changing Climate](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018RG000624).
The review supplies a system-scale numeric span with explicit continuity
limits. Its [admission candidate](../research/antarctic-slope-current-admission-candidate.json)
records the passage, measurement scope, citation receipt, proposed taxonomy,
and integration requirements. It is the tenth canonical ranked value, at rank 2; scientific claim review remains pending.

The same paper already supplies regional shelf/slope regime classification.
Preserve that as a cited, source-scoped vocabulary; do not describe it as an
OSW invention or assign a permanent warm/cold label to the entire current.
Its criteria require hydrographic depth and regional support, not NASA surface
animation or the thermal sign of an eddy core.

### Excluded tempting corroboration: California Current

A search also found Saldívar-Lucio et al.'s 2016 macro-scale upwelling paper
([UCSD publication copy](https://ccelter.ucsd.edu/wp-content/uploads/bibliodocs/0387ccelter.pdf)).
Its study-area text associates northern Vancouver Island with approximately
42 degrees north, which is geographically inconsistent with the
[Canadian place-name record](https://geonames.nrcan.gc.ca/search-place-names/unique?id=JBRIN)
locating the island's reference point near 49.6 degrees north. The reference
point is not a current endpoint. Do not copy that
latitude as an endpoint or use it as independent validation of a current-axis
length. The existing California rank is explicitly the separately sourced
PICES current-system extent. No ranked value, route, or endpoint is changed by
this additional paper.


## Admitted: Malvinas conference extent

[EGU2009-12242](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2009/EGU2009-12242.pdf)
explicitly supplies a 2,000 km northward-loop estimate. The source is a
conference abstract; layer, axis, endpoint coordinates and error interval are
not supplied. It is the eleventh ranked source estimate, rank 9, with a
conference scope warning. The separate 2009 journal paper was checked for
flow context and does not supply this number. No source route or physical
state join follows from this admission.
