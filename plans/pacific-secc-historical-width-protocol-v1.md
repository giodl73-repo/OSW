# Pacific SECC historical width descriptor protocol v1

2026-10-13. Preserve the 300–900 km range attributed by Rotschi, Hisard and
Jarrige (1972) to the geostrophic South Equatorial Countercurrent near 10 S,
at 170 E (printed p10 / PDF13). It is a historical local width description,
not a whole-current representative width or an annual min/max.

The report reviews eleven Bora/Cyclone cruises between November 1965 and
May 1968. The width endpoints are not assigned to individual occupations.
Keep those cruise dates as study context only, with width observation dates,
calendar-month membership and numerical measurement uncertainty unknown.
Do not create a midpoint, matching seasonal pair or inferred monthly curve.

Keep the near-10 S SECC separate from the South Tropical Countercurrent
south of 15 S and the subsurface/equatorial Cromwell extensions. Contemporary
Pacific SECC naming context uses the published Wu et al. (2026) abstract;
full article methods were not obtained. No contemporary range or depth is
transferred into this historical record.

The source uses geostrophic sections south of 5 S. Its statement that speeds
above 10 cm/s are generally confined to the upper 300 m is not a fixed-depth
width definition. The 500 m no-motion reference describes direct current-meter
methods for equatorial stations, not the geostrophic width operator. Leave the
geostrophic reference level and exact paired-edge criterion unresolved.

Retain the exact 120-page original and acquisition SHA outside Git. Preserve
reviewed title, methods, cruise context and descriptor locators. Annotate only
source facts; no original figures or curves redistributed. The static interval
chart has no midpoint or occupied map width. No route buffer, area, state
containment, NASA identity, width ranking or seasonal geometry is inferred.

The general width protocol applies. Internal validation/role review permits
editorial evidence delivery; canonical scientific admission remains separate.
