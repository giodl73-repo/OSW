# Hiri and East Adriatic local measurement protocol

2026-10-13. Extends the existing width protocol with source-specific editorial
annotations. No whole-current or canonical scientific admission.

## Objects and naming

Hughes (1993), introduction p13 / PDF29, calls the subject of chapter 3 the Hiri
Current. Chapter 3 instead uses NGCU for the broader Coral Sea cyclonic flow,
presuming connectivity to Vitiaz Strait (p45 / PDF61). This batch assigns only
the southern Papua/Rossel study reaches to Hiri under that author's historical
convention. It does not establish an alias for the modern north-New-Guinea
undercurrent, transfer dimensions to that record, or equate the whole historical
circulation with a modern uniform-depth surface Hiri current.

The July 1987 TEW287 A87 section has a reported e-folding scale of about 50 km
(p70 / PDF86; Figure3.15 on p69 / PDF85). July 1990 Fr0690 eastern B90 has a
reported e-folding scale of 75 km (p73 / PDF89; Figure3.18 on p75 / PDF91).
These are author-reported cross-section decay scales, not full edge-to-edge
widths, half-widths, standard deviations, Gaussian widths or numerical errors.
Do not multiply them by two or another theoretical factor. The source's typical
70 km summary, 120 km coastal confinement,160 km core offset, velocity values
and transport estimates remain different descriptors; none is an annual margin.

The two July records differ in year and reach. No 50-75 km annual/seasonal range,
same-site trend, continuous three-year history or playback is admitted. Month
precision is preserved; exact occupation days and fixed width layers are unknown.
Thermal-wind sections, reference-velocity assumptions and the transport's 1500 dbar
integration level do not establish absolute measured velocity edges or a width
at every depth. The1990 core depth 350 m is context, not an assigned width layer.

Pasaric, Mihanovic and Orlic (2019), CIESM congress abstract volume 42 p38 /
PDF38, reports the East Adriatic inflow occupying approximately the first 10 km
offshore during cruise C1 (24-28 May 2017). The authors identify the inflow as
part of EAC. It is a local shore-to-inflow extent along the Primosten southwest
section, not a flow-normal full current width. Instrument sampling10-70 m with
4 m bins does not establish paired width edges for every depth. Figure 1 vectors
are drawn at the surface but represent 10 m; their maximum ~0.3 m/s is not a
new independent EAC speed observation. The plotted C1 section is28 May 2017;
the prose extent is associated with the cruise, with exact width occupation day
unresolved. Cruise C2 (4-8 June 2018) reports a different circulation without a
numeric EAC width. The 20 km pycnocline/upwelling extent and C2's 10 km onshore
pycnocline rise are different objects, and provide no second width or annual range.
