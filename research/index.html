Research object and evidence boundary

A map for asking better ocean-heat questions.

OSW tests whether a fluid-geography vocabulary—reservoirs, pathways, anomalies, buried layers, fronts, and gates—can organize observations without confusing temperature, heat content, and transport.

01 · QUESTION

What is the atlas testing?

The organizing hypothesis is that ocean heat becomes easier to reason about when maps separate three physically different objects:

  1. StorageHeat content integrated through a declared volume.
  2. TransportHeat flux through a declared section, requiring velocity and temperature.
  3. AnomalyDeparture from a declared climatology at a declared depth and time.

Current limit: Atlas 08 demonstrates the visual grammar and co-located surface inspection. It does not yet diagnose a full-depth heat budget or validate the twelve original conceptual footprints.

03 · REPRODUCTION RECEIPT

What exact bytes are displayed?

The three surface layers are final NOAA/NCEI OISST v2.1 AVHRR-only fields for 2026-08-01, retrieved 2026-08-21 14:00 UTC. Native 0.25° data are sampled every eighth cell onto a 90 × 180, 2° display grid. Missing and land values remain explicit. Dataset DOI: 10.25921/RE9P-PT57.

The depth ladder is the July 2026 Scripps RG Argo monthly extension, retrieved 2026-08-28 19:02:31 UTC. It samples the native 1° potential- temperature anomaly at 10, 300, 700, and 1000 dbar every second grid cell. Its climatological reference is the RG 2019 mean and annual cycle derived from 2004–2018 Argo data. The 64.5°S limit excludes the Antarctic shelf and ice cavities.

04 · LITERATURE SPINE

Which papers carry the argument?

Front definitions

Orsi, Whitworth & Nowlin (1995)

Hydrographic definitions of the major ACC fronts; climatological paths are not fixed material contours.

Multiple jets

Sokolov & Rintoul (2009)

Circumpolar fronts branch, merge, diverge, and respond to bathymetry.

Observed exchange

Gutierrez-Villanueva et al. (2020)

Upper-ocean eddy heat flux crosses the ACC in Drake Passage and varies in time.

Regional barriers

Thompson et al. (2018)

Cross-slope exchange depends on eddies, tides, overflows, and regional dynamics.

Coupled counterfactual

England et al. (2017)

Drake closure produces large modeled changes mediated by ice–atmosphere feedbacks.

Contrary constraint

Goldner, Herold & Huber (2014)

Gateway state alone does not explain Eocene–Oligocene circulation change.

Heat observation

Palmer et al. (2019)

Regional heat storage requires depth-resolved, uncertainty-aware observing coverage.

System synthesis

Bennetts et al. (2024)

Connects Southern Ocean circulation, cryosphere, mixing, waves, and their unresolved feedbacks.

Depth-product method

Roemmich & Gilson (2009)

Defines the Argo-only mean, annual cycle, and objective-mapping approach behind the RG pressure-level product.

The original 15-source mechanism audit is available in the literature map; the implemented Argo product adds its method paper here.

05 · QUANTITATIVE PATH

What would turn the map into a heat-budget study?

1

Resolve vertical structure

Extend the four-level anomaly ladder toward a full profile and salinity, with uncertainty and coverage.

2

Diagnose transport

Define sections and integrate velocity-weighted heat flux using a dynamically consistent state estimate.

3

Test gates and barriers

Add bathymetry, sea ice, fronts, and controlled perturbations rather than inferring barriers from color.

4

Compare budgets

Close regional storage, advection, mixing, and surface-flux terms with propagated uncertainty.

Predeclared tests and stop conditions are in Predictions and Causal Heat Experiments.
