EXPERIMENT 01 · PROJECTION BAKEOFF

Where should the
world ocean begin?

Most world maps preserve continents by cutting the ocean. This experiment reverses that priority and asks which projection best preserves the geography of reservoirs, anomalies, currents, and gates.

Leading hypothesisPELAGOS

Equal-area, ocean-led, and deliberately weakest over the Sahara—not yet adopted as the atlas default.

The test

  1. 01Do not cut a major ocean pathway.
  2. 02Preserve area when comparing heatmass scale.
  3. 03Keep the warm pool, Arctic approaches, and ACC legible together.
  4. 04Put unavoidable distortion and seams over land.

EXPERIMENT 03 · PROVINCE ATLAS

What if the ocean read like a country?

This reference cartogram turns 56 classic Longhurst surface-province identities into the primary geography. Eleven organizational realms and 22 contiguous schematic regions are provisional OSW constructs layered over those identities. The pieces fill the ocean like states, while continents become empty lakes whose real coastlines belong to the provinces that touch them.

Schematic states, real coast edges

ALSK inherits Alaska and HUMB inherits Peru/Chile. Internal borders come from approximate geographic seeds—not published Longhurst geometry—and do not support measurement.

The memorable layer: four-letter province codes work like state abbreviations. Hover or keyboard-focus any province in the full-size SVG for its complete name.

The hierarchy: heavy borders identify eleven organizational realms, medium borders, codes, and distinct fills identify 22 contiguous schematic regions, and fine lines divide 56 classic province identities. “Contiguous” refers to the unmasked, pre-projection seed topology; land and lobe seams can separate visible pieces. These OSW groupings are provisional, and real ecological boundaries migrate.

The edition layer: this experiment explicitly uses the classic 56-code vocabulary, including separate OCAL and CCAL California provinces. The later 54-province revision should become a separate switch—not an invisible correction.

EXPERIMENT 05 · DATA THROUGH THE STATES

Now make the map carry evidence.

The same Oceanic Mollweide plate now carries seven existing Atlas fields: absolute SST, SST anomaly, estimated SST analysis error, and four Argo anomaly depths. The gridded field is always primary. Ocean-state borders are a reference overlay—never a replacement for the source grid and never an implied state average.

Absolute temperatureThe broad tropical reservoir becomes unmistakable, while state borders give recognizable names to the gradient without quantizing it.

Anomaly and errorPatchier fields reveal whether the hierarchy still helps when color no longer follows latitude—and whether an apparent signal sits where analysis error is elevated.

Depth and absenceThe Argo ladder exposes subsurface structure and its coverage boundary. Blank polar water is shown as missing coverage, not invented data.

Reading contract: cells retain their source values and are projected directly. Borders and codes support orientation only. No value is averaged by state, no state is assigned one temperature, and none of these fields is heat content or transport.

MOTION LADDER · ZONING OFF

Let the water draw first.

Before revising the 11/22/56 hierarchy, OSW removes it completely and studies the velocity field on its own. M1 maps direction and speed; M2 integrates declared surface pathways; M3 joins native full-depth velocity, temperature, and face geometry at one gate. Each level keeps its own claim boundary.

What appearsEquatorial jets, gyre circulation, boundary-current acceleration, and circumpolar motion emerge as one connected texture.

What changesThe seasonal plate reveals reversals and migrations hidden by one annual mean. It remains a single historical year—not a climatology.

Motion skeletonThe holds/turns plate separates aligned corridors from seasonal turning continuously. Its candidate counts are declared diagnostics—not new regions.

Boundary auditThe frozen 22-region overlay asks where seasonal surface motion crosses a border and where motion inside a region disagrees. It ranks questions; it does not redraw the map.

Orientation verdictA diverging comparison restores the other half of the question: cyan favors motion along a border, coral favors motion across it, and neutral means oblique or mixed—not “unknown.”

Partition sensitivityA zoning-free threshold bakeoff tests whether connected seasonal directions settle near a natural region count. Counts and retained coverage move sharply, so no preferred number is promoted.

Method robustnessAt the same threshold, allowing diagonal neighbors changes 20 sizable components into 9. Leave-one-season-out tests stay near 20–21, exposing graph topology—not an ocean law—as the dominant count control.

Region passportsEvery frozen region receives the same five diagnostics without collapsing them into a ranking. A dash means no screened border evidence, preserving the difference between “unsupported” and “zero.”

Membership auditThe 56 province signatures reveal which members inside a region agree or oppose one another. All-pairs comparisons diagnose containers; they are not adjacency tests or automatic split instructions.

Internal seamsThe adjacency correction maps only province pairs that touch on the sampled grid. This removes distant all-pairs exaggeration and locates the internal interfaces that genuinely deserve more study.

Seam seasonsThe same owned seams are tested independently in DJF, MAM, JJA, and SON. None remains opposed in all four seasons, preventing a seasonal reversal from becoming a permanent-looking split line.

Drake pathwaysFour 10-parcel surface ensembles use the native regional grid and a strict wet-stencil RK4 solver. Coastal and domain losses remain visible; tracks diagnose possible kinematic routes, never transported volume or heat.

Release neighborhoodsEach central seed becomes a deterministic 3×3 ±15 km neighborhood. Most trials preserve the broad eastward corridor, but endpoint clouds expose local divergence that one authoritative line would hide.

Release windowsEach seed also starts from five dates spanning ten days. Completion remains broadly stable while endpoints fan out, showing that a corridor can persist even when one exact forecast-like line does not.

Paired corridor passportPosition and clock support remain two adjacent diagnostics at every seed. Thirty-two of 40 are full under both tests; the exceptions concentrate at the passage margins.

Paired Arctic entrancesA whole-Fram surface average hides northward Atlantic-side motion inside stronger western export. Separating Fram’s opposing lanes and the eastward Barents route establishes the geometry required before full-depth heat transport.

Arctic screen challengeWestern Fram export and Barents inflow keep their signs across every nearby case. Eastern Fram motion reverses north of 79°N, so the M2 line cannot select the final full-depth gate.

Arctic corridorsWestern Fram forms the cleanest deterministic export corridor; eastern Fram splits between northward and other fates; Barents is predominantly eastward. Equal particle counts and terminated tracks are visible diagnostics, not probability, volume, or heat.

Arctic native geometryFram aligns with one 60-face land-bounded V row. The Barents U column drifts 10.47°, and Bear Island is wet at this resolution. An observational proxy and a land-bounded model closure must therefore remain separate gates.

Barents section bakeoffA 43-face wet-ended Fugløya–Bear proxy and a 73-face coast-to-coast Norway–Svalbard closure both pass the same native topology checks. Valid geometry cannot make their endpoints or measured domains equivalent.

Barents construction sensitivityAcross 54 cases, three path-cost scales leave every fixed-endpoint route unchanged. Endpoint intent creates nine proxy paths and six closure paths, so later transport must carry an endpoint sensitivity rather than a decorative line.

Arctic full-depth geometryFram opens 814 km² and reaches 2,577 m, while the two Barents definitions open 161 and 224 km² and remain shallower than 700 m. Area constrains future transport but is not velocity or heat.

Arctic transport pilotFebruary native T/S/U/V resolves +6.21/−6.66 Sv opposing Fram branches and +3.23/+3.64 Sv net through the two Barents definitions. The 0.41 Sv gap is a domain effect; one month is not climatology or heat convergence.

Arctic seasonal snapshotsFram exports net water but retains positive 0°C-reference advective heat in all four sampled months because its northward branch is warmer. Both Barents definitions import water and heat; four snapshots are not an annual mean.

Indonesian gate readinessThe coast is part of the measurement. Lombok and Ombai are underresolved on the native OSCAR grid; Makassar and Lifamatola retain points but oppose the declared route at the surface; only Timor supplies a usable M2 direction hint.

Indonesian grid bakeoffPublic 1/12° HYCOM restores candidate wet support at all five passages and exposes their distinct vertical profiles. Resolution passes; one collocated daily field still cannot supply native-face volume or heat transport.

Agulhas junctionThe southwestward coastal current, energetic retroflection, eastward return current, and variable Cape Basin leakage occupy distinct motion roles. Low net coherence in the turning field means changing direction—not still water.

Agulhas fate challengeOne hundred eight source-tagged surface pathways leave most outcomes unresolved, while nine reasonable gate pairs yield nine different Cape/return censuses. Equal particle counts cannot become a leakage percentage.

Agulhas M3 contractPublished methods require transport-weighted full-depth releases, four-year three-dimensional paths, exact GoodHope geometry, and a complete crossing log. All crossings and odd crossings answer different questions.

Published Agulhas replicationThe authors' exact bent West+Northwest exit replaces OSW's provisional straight gate. Recalculation from 57 archived release years recovers 9.894 Sv mean leakage and +0.464 Sv per decade; this is attributed INALT20/Parcels model output, not a new OSW run or observation.

Native heat gateFour sampled 2018 ORAS5 months use one 75-level, 87-face Drake gate. Net volume is steadier than its opposing branches, while heat transport visibly changes with reference temperature.

Gate method challengeFive nearby native gates keep their four-sample volume means within 0.111 Sv, while offline temperature collocation moves the 0°C-reference heat mean by up to 0.050 PW. These are separate method sensitivities, not an uncertainty score.

Vertical anatomyNearly half of net volume occurs below 700 m, while roughly two thirds of the 0°C-reference net heat signal occurs above it. The flow is full-depth; temperature weighting makes the heat portrait shallower.

Thermal populationsMost westward counterflow is at or below 2°C, while eastward flow spans all six declared classes. Temperature alone does not establish water-mass identity.

Native gate portraitThe actual latitude–depth slice locates the cold Antarctic shelf, deep basin, warmer northern wedge, broad eastward current, and embedded westward lanes. Dots and crosses encode motion normal to the page.

Native-cell accountingMatched volume and heat maps expose the alternating lanes that create the net gate result. Cold southern and deep cells fade under temperature weighting; cell brightness remains resolution-dependent.

First control volumeA small four-sided native box balances western inflow against eastern and northern exits to within 0.007 Sv. Reference ambiguity nearly collapses, but the remaining heat divergence is not accumulation without storage and non-advective terms.

Control-box challengeEight nested boxes all remain volume-balanced below 0.010 Sv, while heat divergence changes by 0.034–0.053 PW with extent. The residual belongs to declared geometry, not “Drake Passage” universally.

Storage probeNative T-cell area and partial thickness expose cooling through August and slight warming by November. Sparse tendencies reduce but do not close the budget; the remainder is not assigned to a heat source.

Monthly budget probeTwelve compact native states exactly reproduce four prior anchors and resolve eleven tendencies. The 334-day mean remains +0.0622 PW unclosed, so denser sampling does not replace missing physics.

Native surface forcingThe matched ORCA025 net-downward surface term supplies +0.0195 PW and explains 31% of the prior mean remainder. +0.0427 PW remains unresolved in every interval; model forcing is not direct observation or closure.

METHOD FIREWALL

New viewpoint,
established mathematics.

PELAGOS is an experimental aspect and cut policy, not a newly derived projection equation. It uses spherical Lambert azimuthal equal-area centered at 20°S, 165°W:

+proj=laea +lat_0=-20 +lon_0=-165 +R=1

Its antipode—and unavoidable singular boundary—is 20°N, 15°E. Placing that point in the Sahara moves the projection's greatest failure away from the Pacific warm pool, the Southern Ocean ring, and most major exchange pathways.

All four global maps use the same irregular geographic heat polygons, inset shelf contours, transient anomaly, pathways, gates, and checksum-pinned Natural Earth 1:110m coastlines. The polygon boundaries are schematic—not observed thresholds. These are projection tests, not heat observations or transport calculations.

The Province Atlas is different by design: its provinces are original approximate nearest-seed geometry built from the classic Longhurst vocabulary, not transformed geographic province polygons. Public-domain Natural Earth land is subtracted from the province field, making each visible coastal edge part of the province that reaches it. The view supports geographic recognition and comparison of named parts, but no measurement of province area, boundary position, distance, direction, or transport. The static province system is a mean ecological reference whose natural boundaries move seasonally and interannually.
